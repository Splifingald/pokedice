      PAGE 1 OF THE UX PASS · HOME

      Home

      One big button to play, your team on show, everything else one tap away. It replaces the Map page as the place you land.

      1. Top barYou, your badges, your gold. The + opens the Shop; the cup opens the leaderboard.
      2. Where you areArea, level range, rounds, species caught here. Tap it for the area’s details: gym, legendary and when it shows up, one-time finds still there, Pokémon to catch with rarity and levels, what a round brings.
      3. Your team, roamingThe three of them (a team is three at most) wander the area’s own scenery. Friends walk over to each other and hop, little hearts float up; Lapras starts a song and the others join, notes rising. Tap one: it hops, a heart pops, its card shows. A tired one sweats and naps.
      4. ContinueThe only big button, in thumb reach, and it says what comes next.
      5. AreasA sheet, not a page. Search by area or by Pokémon (“Pikachu” finds where it lives), filter To catch, Secret or Cleared, sort by route, level or most to catch. A card opens the area’s details; GO travels. The outline is the state: orange where you are, green cleared, grey locked. Each area has one picture: the scene the team roams, and cards show its middle. Regions, next to the region name, switches region: the next one appears once the League is won, starters included. The Areas button has three states: plain, a NEW dot, gold “New region”.
      6. WidgetsVersus joins once three Pokémon reach Lv.50: Set team first, then Fight. The newest secret area travels in one tap; when there is none, the next one shows its progress. The Day Care shows who is ready and collects in place.
      7. Tab barHome in the middle. Dots only for things you can act on: affordable upgrades, a new Pokédex entry.

      Accessible by default

      • Every control is a real button, 44 px or more; the team is also a keyboard list of buttons.
      • Both sheets (Areas, an area’s details) are dialogs: focus moves in, stays in, Esc closes the top one, focus comes back.
      • Text is navy on paper (11:1); status is never colour alone (chips carry words).
      • With reduced motion on, the team stands still and nothing pulses.

      UXMenus and retention

      Players leave at the menu

      Many players uninstall within a day and a quarter of them cite confusing interfaces; key controls belong in the thumb zone. Take: one action per screen, at the bottom.

      Pixel-true, frame-timed, battle-first

      Every effect is drawn at the stage's own resolution: light and smoke are ordered-dithered, flashes are white silhouettes, and contact always gets a hit-stop. The HUD around the stage follows the style picked at the top. Slow it to ¼ speed or step frame by frame to read the timing.
