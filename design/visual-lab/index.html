      THE UX PASS · HOME AND ITS TABS

      Home

      One big button to play, your team on show, everything else one tap away. It replaces the Map page as the place you land. Every screen it leads to is built: the tab bar (Team, Pokédex, Poké Mart, Upgrades), the battle, the Day Care, Versus, the leaderboard and the trainer card.

      1. Top barYou (your look), your badges, your gold. The avatar opens the trainer card, the gold opens the Shop, the cup opens the leaderboard.
      2. Where you areArea, level range, rounds, species caught here. Tap it for the area’s details: gym, legendary and when it shows up, one-time finds still there, Pokémon to catch with rarity and levels, what a round brings.
      3. Your team, roamingThe three of them (a team is three at most) wander the area’s own scenery. Friends walk over to each other and hop, little hearts float up; Lapras starts a song and the others join, notes rising. Tap one: it hops, a heart pops, its card shows. A tired one sweats and naps.
      4. ContinueThe only big button, in thumb reach, and it says what comes next.
      5. AreasA sheet, not a page. Search by area or by Pokémon (“Pikachu” finds where it lives), filter To catch, Secret or Cleared, sort by route, level or most to catch. A card opens the area’s details; GO travels. The outline is the state: orange where you are, green cleared, grey locked. Each area has one picture: the scene the team roams, and cards show its middle. Regions, next to the region name, switches region: the next one appears once the League is won. Enter starts the partner pick: in the professor’s lab, three Poké Balls drop onto the table, a tap opens one, and “Do you want to pick it?” comes with its type, what it hits hard, what hits it hard and its dice; no sends it back into its ball. The Areas button has three states: plain, a NEW dot, gold “New region”.
      6. WidgetsTwo by two. The newest secret area travels in one tap; when there is none, the next one shows its progress. The Day Care shows your two with their level and XP, the friends’ count and the next Egg check; it opens the Day Care and never acts on its own. With an Egg waiting it turns gold, an Egg shakes in place of the Pokémon, and a tap opens the Day Care straight into the hatching. Locked, it counts to 20 Pokémon caught, once for every region. Versus sits under the secret area: locked, it shows how close you are (3 Pokémon at Lv.50); then Set team, then Fight. Under the Day Care, an empty slot waits for special events.
      7. Tab barHome in the middle. New 16×16 icons: a Mart bag, a die going up, three balls for the team, the red Pokédex; a podium for the leaderboard. Dots only for things you can act on: affordable upgrades, a new Pokédex entry.

      The tabs

      1. TeamThe three as cards: sprite, HP, XP, dice. Drag one onto another to change who goes out first; the lead wears gold. The Box below: search, sort by number, level or newest, filter by type. A card opens the Pokémon: stats, every die face, what it learns next, items to use. Swaps stay a Pokémon Center thing, said once at the top.
      2. Pokédex151 tiles, silhouettes for the missing ones. Filters All, Caught, Missing and Nearby (catchable in an open area). A number finds anything; a name only what you’ve caught. A missing one shows where it lives, with the same GO as the Areas list.
      3. Poké MartBuy and Sell, category chips, Balls first. A row opens to pick ×1, ×5 or ×10; the button gives the total, or how much is missing. Items that need more badges wait under “Coming later”.
      4. UpgradesCombos and Dice. Each card: example dice (or the die’s six faces), a 10-pip track, the bonus now → next, the price. Status faces are dark tiles with their icon, and say what they do with the game’s numbers. Affordable narrows to what you can buy; the tab bar’s dot counts it. Locked ones say why.

      Accessible by default

      • Every control is a real button, 44 px or more; the team is also a keyboard list of buttons. Team order works without dragging too (Make lead).
      • Both sheets (Areas, an area’s details) are dialogs: focus moves in, stays in, Esc closes the top one, focus comes back.
      • Text is navy on paper (11:1); status is never colour alone (chips carry words).
      • With reduced motion on, the team stands still and nothing pulses.

      The screens off Home

      1. BattleFrom CONTINUE, full screen. The dice roll by themselves; tap the ones to throw again, then ATTACK. Under the tray, the combo, the sum, the type multiplier and the status faces, with the engine’s rules. The move is the Animations tab’s timeline; the foe rolls its own dice. Bag (one item a turn), switches, and a free switch after a K.O. The catch happens in the scene: each ball shows its chance, and one throw decides.
      2. Day CareOne for every region, open from 20 caught. Laid out like Home: the yard on top with everyone roaming (pairs seek each other out, hearts fly), no play button. Below, your two (XP toward the next level, Lv.100 the only cap, TAKE BACK) and four friends’ slots: pick a Pokémon from a friend’s Day Care, it visits and nothing changes for the friend. The picker tags who can pair: Compatible with Eevee, with a heart in that Pokémon’s colour. Real Egg groups decide; Ditto pairs with everyone but legendaries, slower. Every 12 h (Ditto: 24 h) a compatible pair leaves an Egg, random as before, shiny 1 in 100; Egg now skips the wait for ₽200 and hatches it on the spot. The name shows once, in the yard’s plate; your two and the four friends’ slots are cards in two columns. The preview bar runs either check on demand.
      3. VersusFrom its widget. Locked, it shows who’s closest to Lv.50. My team: three Lv.50 clones, in order. Opponents: search, To beat / Beaten, and how many of theirs you hit super effectively. FIGHT: the trainer sends out three in turn, both sides on auto; SKIP jumps to the result, decided when the fight starts. A board for Attack and Defense.
      4. LeaderboardFrom the cup. Max level, Progression, Pokédex, Shiny. Your rank sits on top (tap it to find your row); trainers who maxed a board out go to the Hall of Fame.
      5. Trainer cardFrom the avatar. Your look (the game’s trainer sprites, the one others see), name, money, Pokédex, best level, areas, shinies, Versus record; the badge case with the crown; the menu.
